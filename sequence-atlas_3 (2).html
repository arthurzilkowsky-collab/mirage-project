Sequence Atlas

Paste a sequence — digits of π, DNA, or anything else — and it gets split every time two identical symbols land next to each other. Play the whole thing (with a rest at each split), or click any one group to isolate it and evolve it using Schillinger-style transformations: retrograde, circular permutation, inversion, augmentation/diminution.

Mirror groups — each block ends where a symbol repeats; the teal ring shows nested palindrome depth

These are Schillinger's own devices for evolving a short cell into new material: circular permutation rotates it, retrograde reverses it, inversion flips its melodic contour around its own axis, and augmentation/diminution stretches or compresses its durations. Combine retrograde with inversion for a classic "retrograde inversion."

Interference — a resultant rhythm from two generators

Two generators — think of them as two clocks ticking at different rates, started together. Every point where either clock ticks becomes a beat; the gaps between beats become the resultant rhythm. Where both clocks land on the same beat, you hear both groups at once. Voice B plays an octave up so the two stay distinguishable. The generator values default to the two groups' lengths, but you can type any integers you like — that's how Schillinger used the technique.

This is a structural view, not a statistical or predictive one — it shows where repeats occur and how symbols nest, nothing more. "Coherence" here just means fewer, longer groups.
